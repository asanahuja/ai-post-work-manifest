# AI-Assisted Post-Work Civilization Manifesto

> An evolving theoretical and academic thought experiment. This document is not a prediction, a scientific consensus or a closed political programme. It is an interdisciplinary framework for thinking about how advanced artificial intelligence, automation, universal basic services, democratic governance, sustainability and scientific progress could transform human civilization.

## 1. Starting point

Artificial intelligence should not be limited to optimizing the current economic and social system.

If AI becomes a cognitive infrastructure far beyond individual human capabilities, its deepest potential would not merely be higher productivity, but helping redesign the material conditions of human life.

> **What kind of civilization could we build if we used AI to help guarantee a dignified, free and sustainable life for all humanity?**

## 2. Dignity and basic needs

A person's survival and dignity should not necessarily depend on selling their labour.

A technologically advanced civilization should aim to guarantee at least food, dignified housing, water, essential energy, healthcare, education, connectivity, basic mobility and access to knowledge.

These form a basis for **Universal Basic Services (UBS)**.

The goal is not identical outcomes for everyone, but ensuring that no one is excluded from the minimum conditions of a dignified life.

## 3. AI as universal infrastructure

A super-capable AI should not be concentrated exclusively in the hands of a few companies, states or owners.

Its capabilities could be treated as fundamental civilizational infrastructure.

A universal access layer could support education, healthcare, knowledge, research, public administration, agriculture, energy, logistics, territorial planning and resource management.

A distinction is required between:

### Universal access to cognitive capacity

and

### Restricted access to actions capable of systemic harm

> **Maximum scientific and cognitive capability does not imply absence of governance.**

## 4. Democracy and governance

AI should not govern humanity.

Humans should continue to define rights, principles, objectives, limits and social priorities.

AI could act as simulator, adviser, coordinator, prediction system, resource planner, verification tool and scientific infrastructure.

> **Humanity decides what it considers a good civilization. AI helps calculate how to make it possible.**

## 5. Equality does not mean uniformity

A more egalitarian society does not require eliminating individual diversity.

The relevant equality concerns rights, access and dignity, reduction of structural domination and real freedom to choose one's own life.

People would still have different vocations, interests and goals.

The difference is that those choices would be less constrained by immediate survival.

## 6. A post-work society

Automation should not have cost reduction as its only goal.

Its deeper potential is:

> **to reduce the amount of compulsory human labour required to sustain civilization.**

If robots, AI and automated systems can produce a significant share of food, energy, housing, transport, services, manufacturing, software and knowledge, we can begin to separate:

> **working**

from

> **having the right to live with dignity**

This does not mean people stop doing things. It means more people can devote themselves to what they consider valuable.

## 7. The problem is not only money

Redistributing money does not automatically remove scarcity.

If the same housing, food, energy and resources exist but everyone has more money, scarce resources remain scarce.

Therefore we must distinguish:

> **money**

from

> **real resources**

Structural transformation requires increasing sustainable material abundance and guaranteeing access to essential resources.

## 8. Resource economy

A future society could manage different categories of resources with different mechanisms.

### Basic needs

They could be partly or fully outside market allocation.

### Common infrastructure

Energy, compute, AI, data, scientific knowledge, networks, healthcare infrastructure and essential productive systems could be treated as public, common or cooperative infrastructure.

### Scarce or optional resources

Markets, queues, lotteries, priority rules, contribution systems or other allocation mechanisms could still be used.

> **Markets should not determine access to the basic conditions of a dignified life.**

## 9. Beyond wages

An advanced society could recognize contribution in forms other than traditional wages:

- time
- care
- community contribution
- research
- teaching
- volunteering
- innovation
- cultural creation
- shared knowledge

Time credits, social recognition, reputation systems or other contribution units could be explored.

These mechanisms should not simply become a new currency reproducing the same concentrations of power.

## 10. Healthcare as a universal right

Healthcare is a clear example of the paradigm shift.

Universal health infrastructure should cover prevention, pediatrics, mental health, general medicine, specialties, rehabilitation, surgery, reconstruction after accidents and treatments needed to restore quality of life.

AI could help predict needs, detect disease, personalize treatment, allocate resources, support diagnosis and accelerate research.

## 11. The ownership question

One of the most important questions is not only:

> What will AI be able to do?

but:

> **Who will own the productive capacity created by AI?**

If AI multiplies productivity but that infrastructure is owned exclusively by a minority, inequality may increase.

If a significant share is public, cooperative or common infrastructure, productivity gains could translate into less compulsory work, more universal services, more free time, more education, more research and more individual freedom.

## 12. A digital twin of Earth

A sufficiently capable AI could maintain a dynamic representation of the planet.

A **planetary digital twin** could integrate climate, population, agriculture, energy, biodiversity, water, materials, infrastructure, transport, healthcare, production and consumption.

It could help answer questions such as:

- How much food will be needed?
- Where will water scarcity emerge?
- Where should energy be produced?
- Which resources should be recycled?
- How much healthcare infrastructure will be needed?
- Where should housing be built?
- Which ecosystems must not be degraded?

Such a system should not decide automatically. It should improve the quality of human decisions.

## 13. Population and sustainability

There is no immutable maximum number of humans that can live on Earth.

Sustainable capacity depends on energy, food, water, technology, efficiency, consumption, materials, biodiversity, territory and the production model.

> **The human limit of the planet is not only demographic, but technological, ecological and social.**

The useful question is:

> **How many people can live with a high quality of life without destroying the ecological systems that make that life possible?**

This question should be addressed without coercion or elimination of people.

## 14. Energy as critical infrastructure

An automated, electrified and AI-assisted civilization requires enormous amounts of energy.

Without abundant energy, much of post-scarcity is impossible.

Solar, wind, nuclear, storage, smart grids, geothermal energy, efficiency, new technologies and fusion if it becomes viable at scale should all be explored.

## 15. Earth before other planets

Space settlement may be part of humanity's future, but it should not become an excuse for abandoning terrestrial sustainability.

One possible sequence is:

1. Sustainable Earth.
2. Partial industrialization of the Solar System.
3. Permanent settlements beyond Earth.
4. Interstellar exploration.

## 16. Physical limits

Not all limits are social or economic. Some are physical.

According to known physics, the speed of light defines a fundamental causal limit.

Quantum mechanics contains extraordinary phenomena, but today we know of no demonstrated mechanism for controlled faster-than-light transmission of information or matter.

This does not mean current science is final.

It means we must distinguish:

- what we know
- what we suspect
- what we do not know

## 17. Fundamental science and super-capable AI

An AI-assisted civilization could devote enormous capacity to fundamental science:

- quantum gravity
- dark energy
- dark matter
- spacetime structure
- new materials
- superconductivity
- quantum computing
- fusion
- advanced propulsion

The objective is not to assume miraculous technology exists, but to radically increase humanity's ability to discover what is physically possible.

## 18. AI as a civilizational accelerator

The goal is not AI replacing humanity.

It is AI helping humanity understand the planet, reduce compulsory work, produce more efficiently, guarantee material rights, accelerate science, reduce inequality, manage resources, anticipate risks and explore space.

> **AI would be the cognitive nervous system of a civilization, not its master.**

## 19. Measuring progress

Success should not be measured only through GDP, productivity, profit or consumption.

It should also include health, healthy life expectancy, real freedom, available time, education, inequality, environmental quality, safety, access to knowledge, participation, life satisfaction and ecological resilience.

The economy should serve human needs.

## 20. Freedom and limits

A system oriented toward the common good must not become totalitarian.

Technology cannot justify absolute surveillance, permanent social control, elimination of dissent, imposition of a single way of life, loss of privacy or absolute concentration of power.

> **An egalitarian society only makes sense if it preserves dignity, freedom, diversity and rights.**

## 21. Beneficial AI by design

AI risks are real, but they should not be confused with inevitable predictions of catastrophe.

Advanced AI intended as social infrastructure should be designed so that its primary function is to expand human wellbeing, freedom, knowledge and sustainability.

Safety cannot depend solely on a hypothetical internal moral consciousness.

It requires defence in depth:

- internal alignment
- permissioning and least privilege
- sandboxing
- tool control
- human oversight
- independent monitoring
- logs and traceability
- audits
- shutdown mechanisms
- reversibility
- separation of duties

Free default access should not be granted to capabilities that could facilitate weapons, biological threats, destructive cyberattacks or sabotage of critical infrastructure.

> **A safe superintelligence is not one that promises to be good, but one that remains safe even when it is wrong.**

## 22. Governance without capture

AI regulation is necessary, but badly designed regulation can reinforce concentration if only large firms can bear compliance costs, access compute or influence rule-making.

AI governance should not be reduced to:

> governments + major technology companies deciding for everyone else.

Depending on context, governance should involve states, independent research, universities, civil society, communities, workers, companies, public institutions and regions with less technological capacity.

We must distinguish:

> **who develops the technology**

from

> **who defines the rules under which that technology affects society.**

If AI becomes essential infrastructure, public, scientific, cooperative or common capacity in compute, models, data and AI services should be explored.

## 23. Central question

If technology allows us to progressively separate survival from compulsory work:

> **what would we want human life to become?**

This question affects economics, philosophy, politics, education, family, science, work, culture and identity.

AI may not only force us to rethink technology.

It may force us to rethink what we mean by civilization.

# Summary principles

1. Human dignity should not depend exclusively on income.
2. Basic needs should tend toward universal rights.
3. Advanced AI should include a universal access layer.
4. Critical AI infrastructure should not be concentrated without democratic control.
5. Automation should reduce compulsory work, not merely eliminate wages.
6. AI-generated productivity should broadly benefit society.
7. Humans define values; AI helps find solutions.
8. Sustainability is a real physical constraint.
9. Sustainable population depends on consumption models and technology, not a fixed number.
10. Earth must become sustainable before relying on space settlement.
11. Science should explore physical limits without turning hypotheses into certainties.
12. Equality cannot justify authoritarianism or removal of individual freedoms.
13. Technology should expand human life options.
14. Progress should be measured in human and ecological wellbeing, not only economic growth.
15. AI safety should rely on defence in depth, not trust in the model alone.
16. AI governance should resist corporate, political and technological capture.

## Final hypothesis

```text
AI + automation + abundant energy
                +
democratic governance + universal rights
                +
sustainable resource management
                +
safety + distributed control
                ↓
less compulsory work
                ↓
more real freedom
                ↓
more time to care, learn, research,
create, explore and participate in society
```

This is not a prediction.

It is a **possible direction for civilizational design**.

> **What will we decide to do with an intelligence capable of helping us redesign civilization?**
