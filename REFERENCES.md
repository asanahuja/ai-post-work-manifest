# References

This file is the living bibliography for factual and research-backed claims used by the manifesto.

The manifesto intentionally combines:

- normative proposals
- empirical observations
- theoretical hypotheses
- speculative scenarios
- open questions

Not every normative statement requires a citation, but factual claims presented as evidence should increasingly be traceable to reliable sources.

## Reference policy

Prefer, in roughly this order:

1. peer-reviewed research and academic books
2. original scientific or technical publications
3. international and public institutions
4. official standards and regulatory texts
5. original documentation from relevant technology organizations
6. high-quality secondary analysis when primary evidence is unavailable

For disputed or uncertain topics, include multiple serious perspectives where useful.

## Current research areas

Future versions should progressively document sources related to:

- AI safety, alignment and loss-of-control research
- AI governance and international regulation
- market concentration in compute, cloud and foundation models
- Universal Basic Services and post-work economics
- automation, labour and productivity
- healthcare access and public infrastructure
- planetary boundaries and ecological sustainability
- energy demand and energy systems
- demographic projections and carrying capacity
- digital twins and planetary-scale modelling
- quantum mechanics, relativity and physical limits
- space settlement and long-term civilization studies

## Citation principle

A source supports a claim; it does not automatically validate the manifesto's normative conclusions.

Where possible, future versions should clearly distinguish:

> **evidence → interpretation → normative proposal**
